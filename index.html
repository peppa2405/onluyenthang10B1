LEARNING BY DOING

A For a long time, most schools taught in the same way: the teacher spoke, and the students listened. Today, many teachers believe that people remember more when they take part in an activity themselves. A student who builds a model bridge, for example, understands how it works much better than one who only reads about it.

B Learning by doing is not a new idea. In the past, young people often learnt a job by working next to an experienced person. A boy who wanted to become a carpenter, for instance, spent several years watching and helping a master before he could make furniture alone.

C Many modern schools now use projects. Students might run a small shop, make a short film or look after a school garden. These tasks require them to plan, to make decisions and to work with each other. Teachers say that students are often more interested in lessons that connect with real life.

D Of course, mistakes are part of this kind of learning. A plan may fail, or a model may break, but students usually try another way instead of giving up. Many experts believe that learning from mistakes helps children become confident and independent.

E Nevertheless, not everything can be learnt by doing. Students still need to read books, listen to lectures and revise for exams, because these activities give them the knowledge that practical work cannot always provide. The best schools combine both methods.
